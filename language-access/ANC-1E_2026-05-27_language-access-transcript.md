## Why this is on file (the actionable takeaways)

- **Document translation IS reimbursable.** Under the ANC Omnibus Act of 2016, OHR reimburses ANCs for interpretation, **document/outreach translation** (minutes, agendas, materials created by the ANC), and assistive listening. **ASL is excluded** (covered by the ADA instead).
- **How to claim:** submit the **ANC Reimbursement Form** + invoices, service quotes, and proof of payment to **laura.gonzalez@dc.gov**. Form: https://ohr.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/ohr/Revised%204.2024.ANC%20Reimbursement%20Form%20%28BLANK%29%201.pdf · Factsheet: https://ohr.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/ohr/ANC%20Reimbusement%20Factsheet%20FY24%20Updated%2009_03_2024.pdf
- **FY26 deadline: October 5, 2026** (don't wait until year-end).
- **Amharic is one of DC's six languages** (Laura named them: Amharic, French, Spanish, Korean, Chinese, Vietnamese).
- **For the Ethiopian/Eritrean community specifically:** she pointed to the Mayor's constituency offices — **Office of African Affairs (OAA)**, plus OLA (Latino Affairs) and MOAPIA (Asian/Pacific Islander) — as partners on "know your rights" outreach for limited-English-proficiency residents.
- **Approved translation vendors:** OHR keeps a **vendors' guide** on its site (citywide-contract vendors — MCIS, AXIS, TransPerfect per the captions; verify spellings). Agencies procure via their own purchase order.
- **In flight:** OHR is working on **amendments to the Language Access Act** (more awareness + enforcement).

**[35:49 — What ANCs can be reimbursed for]**

"Pursuant to the Advisory Neighborhood Commission Omnibus Act of 2016, ANCs can be reimbursed by OHR for costs related to translation and interpretation services, as well as purchase of assistive listening systems — ensuring full participation for residents and commissioners that have limited or no English proficiency.
