Plain language

What the rules actually say

Written for someone who has never heard of a "hosted license." No jargon assumed.

What is legal today. Renting out space short-term in DC requires a short-term rental license. There are two kinds, and which one you need depends on whether you live there.
Hosted license. The home is your primary residence and you're present during the stay — a spare room, a basement flat while you live upstairs. No night cap.
Unhosted (vacation rental) license. The whole place is let while you're away. This is the one a whole-home rental needs — and it's capped at 90 nights a year.
Why the gap matters. A whole home advertising a hosted license while its calendar is open most of the year is the population worth a closer look — not proof of anything, a place to ask the question.
What would change B26-0647 · pending, NOT law The Short-Term Rental Regulation Amendment Act of 2026 would expand short-term rentals, not restrict them: it would let renters operate at their primary residence, let owners license a second property they own (still capped at 90 nights), add a special-event license, and consolidate the hosted and unhosted (vacation-rental) categories above into one. It is before the Council; nothing on this page assumes it has passed.
How to raise a complaint. Concerns go to DC's Department of Licensing and Consumer Protection (DLCP). Your ANC and single-member district — shown on the "Your block" tab — are also where neighbors organize a response.

How to read the numbers

What counts. "Full-time STR" = a whole home (not a spare room), a minimum stay under 30 nights, and the calendar open at least 90 nights a year. Deliberately conservative — it knowingly undercounts.
Where this comes from. Every number on this page is drawn from Airbnb listings only — the Inside Airbnb public snapshot of 24 June 2026. Vrbo, Booking.com and corporate operators such as Sonder and Blueground are not counted, and there is no comparable public dataset for them. Because whole-home lets are exactly what those platforms specialize in, the true figure is higher than what you see here — this page is a floor, not a ceiling.
Four limits, stated not hidden. Airbnb only, as above. This is one day's snapshot (24 June 2026), so no trend is claimed. One host can list the same home twice, so the count is an upper bound on distinct dwellings within Airbnb. Rent benchmarks are estimated in 10 of 39 clusters and marked wherever they appear.
License is a signal, not a verdict. The license shown is self-reported by the host and displayed on the listing. It marks a population worth auditing; it is never a finding that a listing is unlawful, and no host is named.
